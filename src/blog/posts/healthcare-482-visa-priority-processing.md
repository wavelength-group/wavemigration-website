Good news for hospitals, practices and international doctors: healthcare applications for the Skills in Demand visa (subclass 482) will now be given the highest processing priority, and it no longer matters if the doctor lodges from Australia or from abroad.

Our migration team was told of the change directly by the Department of Home Affairs. It follows a new Ministerial Direction signed by Minister Burke, which the Department expects to publish on its website soon.

## In the Department's words

> "Under the new Direction which applies to the subclass 482 program, applications in the healthcare sector will have the highest processing priority, regardless of the applicant's location at time of lodgement."

Director of Temporary Skilled Program Delivery, Department of Home Affairs

## What it means for doctors

- **Lodging from overseas is no longer a disadvantage.** If you're waiting offshore, you can lodge with confidence that your application sits in the top priority group.
- **Priority is not a guarantee of speed.** The Department expects a high volume of priority applications and has said immediate attention will generally be reserved for exceptional circumstances.
- **A complete application still matters most.** Missing documents remain the most common cause of delay, so it pays to have everything ready before you lodge.

## What it means for employers

- **Plan start dates with more confidence.** Offshore recruitment no longer carries the same processing risk for healthcare roles.
- **Keep registration moving at the same time.** Your doctor's AHPRA registration and Medicare provider number still need to progress alongside the visa, so running all three in parallel gives you the best chance of an on-time start.
